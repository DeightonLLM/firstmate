Distributor onboarding works best as a shared readiness exercise. Before the first order, the buyer and supplier can align on who is involved, what is being ordered, where it should go and how questions will be handled.

Cencora's public home page presents distribution, logistics and services for pharmacies, providers and health systems. Anda presents wholesale ordering and customer groups including pharmacies, hospitals and clinics. These are company descriptions of their own services, not a universal onboarding standard or required-document list. ([Cencora](https://www.cencora.com/); [Anda](https://www.andanet.com/))

## Define the relationship and its scope

Identify the account owner and authorized contacts. Clarify which products and services are in scope, which locations may order and how the parties will communicate changes. Confirm specifics directly with the provider rather than assuming a public page describes the arrangement for your account.

## Align the basics before the first order

### Partner identity and locations

Check that both sides understand the relevant organization and delivery locations. Confirm the names, addresses and identifiers used in the systems involved, where applicable.

### Product, ordering and payment information

Agree how the buyer will identify products, place orders and receive confirmations. Establish who handles catalog, account terms and order discrepancies. The exact documents and fields depend on the parties and the service.

### Quality and handling expectations

Discuss the product handling needs that apply to the items being purchased, relevant quality contacts and how questions are escalated. Any license, accreditation or other document request should be based on the buyer's own policy and applicable requirements—not a checklist invented by this article.

## Test the operational handoff

Before relying on a new process, confirm how staff submit an order, receive acknowledgment, track delivery and request help. A small, controlled operational walkthrough can expose unclear contacts or information gaps before they become routine.

## Maintain the relationship record

Keep current contacts, locations and agreed processes in the buyer's approved records. Decide how changes are communicated and who updates the record. This is practical procurement housekeeping, not a claim about a required review frequency.
