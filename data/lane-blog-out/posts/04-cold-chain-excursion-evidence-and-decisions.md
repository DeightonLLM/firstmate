A temperature alert is a reason to investigate. It is not, by itself, a product disposition.

The research base records that McKesson and Morris & Dickson describe cold-chain capabilities in their own distribution materials. Those company pages support the general point that handling capability is part of distribution services; they do not provide product-specific excursion limits or a universal response procedure. ([McKesson](https://www.mckesson.com/business-solutions/pharmaceutical-medical-products-distribution/); [Morris & Dickson](https://www.morrisdickson.com/))

## Protect the product and preserve the evidence

Follow the approved procedure for the product and location. If that procedure calls for placing affected product on hold, use the designated route and prevent an unreviewed disposition. Do not infer a safe exposure window from a generic article or from a photograph of a refrigerator.

### Identify the shipment

Connect the alert to the product and shipment using the identifiers and records available to your team. Record the affected location and the period under review according to your procedure. Do not add a lot, serial or shipment detail that the source record does not support.

### Preserve logger and handling records

Retain relevant monitoring output, device information, timestamps, packaging observations and available custody or delivery records. Record what is known and what remains uncertain. Avoid rewriting or summarizing source readings in a way that loses their original context.

## Get product-specific technical input

Escalate through the quality or manufacturer contact identified by your procedure. Product labeling, validated handling instructions and qualified quality review—not a generic temperature threshold—must guide the assessment. This article does not specify acceptable ranges, durations or release criteria.

## Document the decision

Record who reviewed the evidence, what information they considered, what disposition was authorized and how affected product was handled. Follow the organization's procedures and applicable requirements; the steps and authority may differ by product and setting.

## Close the loop

Once the immediate issue is resolved, route any investigation or corrective follow-up through the approved quality process. A clear record helps the parties understand the event and any next action without implying that one response fits every product.
