Buy Peptides Lab Supplies Science Blog About Contact 0 Buy Peptides Lab Supplies Science Blog About Contact ← Back to blog Synthesis Custom Peptide Synthesis Guide 2026-02-12 10 min read Understanding Solid-Phase Peptide Synthesis (SPPS) Solid-phase peptide synthesis, pioneered by Bruce Merrifield, remains the gold standard for peptide manufacturing. The method involves sequential amino acid coupling on an insoluble resin support, which allows excess reagents to be simply washed away rather than requiring purification after each step. The process follows these steps: 1. **Resin Loading** — The first amino acid is attached to a solid support 2. **Deprotection** — Removes protecting groups that prevent unwanted side reactions 3. **Coupling** — Activates and attaches the next amino acid in the sequence 4. **Capping** — Blocks any unreacted chains to prevent deletion sequences 5. **Cleavage** — Releases the completed peptide from the resin 6. **Purification** — Isolates the target peptide through HPLC 7. **Characterization** — Mass spectrometry and analytical HPLC verify the product Key Considerations for Custom Peptide Straightforward synthesis, high success rates, 2–4 week turnaround **Long peptides (50–100 aa)** — Fragment condensation or advanced strategies, extended timelines **Complex sequences** — Difficult couplings, aggregation-prone stretches, multiple disulfide bonds, cyclic structures Essential Peptide Modifications N-Terminal Modifications **Acetylation** — Blocks N-terminal charge, mimics natural protein processing, improves stability **Biotin** — Enables detection and purification through streptavidin binding **Fluorescein** — Allows imaging and tracking in cells and tissues **Myristoylation** — Provides membrane targeting through lipid anchoring C-Terminal Modifications **Amidation** — Mimics natural C-terminus, often enhances biological activity **AMCA** — Blue fluorescent labeling for dual-labeling studies **P-nitroanilide** — Creates enzyme substrates for chromogenic detection Internal Modifications Non-natural amino acids include D-amino acids (protease resistance), N-methyl amino acids (metabolic stability), and beta-amino acids (unique folding). Post-translational modifications include phosphorylation, glycosylation, acetylation, methylation, and ubiquitination markers. Structural Modifications — Cyclization Options include head-to-tail cyclization, side-chain to side-chain cyclization (disulfide bonds or lactams), and stapled peptides using hydrocarbon bridges. Benefits include enhanced proteolytic stability, reduced conformational flexibility, improved target binding affinity, and better cell permeability. Quality Control: What to Expect A reputable synthesis partner provides: **Mass Spectrometry** — Confirms molecular weight, validates sequence accuracy **HPLC Chromatography** — Demonstrates purity level, quantifies main peak percentage **Certificate of Analysis** — Batch-specific documentation with storage recommendations Optional advanced characterization: amino acid analysis, NMR spectroscopy, circular dichroism, and peptide content determination.
