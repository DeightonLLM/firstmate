## 1. What Core Distro Is

We are a direct pharmaceutical supply distributor for **503A and 503B compounding pharmacies**.
Four product lines, one partner:

1. **Glass vials** (Type I borosilicate, 2 to 100 ml, clear and amber, RTU and RTS, ISO 8362-1)
2. **Rubber stoppers** (bromobutyl and chlorobutyl, 13 mm and 20 mm)
3. **Aluminum flip-off seals** (color-coded)
4. **Automation equipment** (compact fill-finish lines, ±0.5 percent accuracy, ISO 5, 40 to 50 vials / minute)

Regulatory backbone: **FDA-registered Importer of Record, ISO 8362-1, ISO 13485, GMP, full
documentation** (COA, MSDS, TSE/BSE, sterility certificates with every lot).

Tagline: **Vials. Stoppers. Seals. Done. One Partner.**
Phone vanity: **(833) 84-VIALS**.

The buyer is a pharmacy owner or compliance lead. They do not want hype. They want a steady,
qualified supplier that does not break their audit.

## 2. Brand System (lock to these)

### Colors

| Role | Approx hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Lime accent | `#84E85C` | CTAs, badge outlines, highlight text on dark, single accent on light |
| Forest dark | `#1F3F2C` | Primary dark surface, hero overlays, footer |
| Forest deeper | `#16281B` | Deepest sections, footer bottom |
| Mint off-white | `#F4F8F1` | Light mode background |
| Muted grey-green | `#5A6B5C` | Body copy on dark |
| Pure white | `#FFFFFF` | Headline text on dark |

Rule: **one accent only per composition. If you use lime, it should pop. If the whole image is lime,
nothing pops.**

### Typography

- **Display / headers:** Barlow Condensed Bold (700, 800). All-caps. Tight letter-spacing (0.02 to 0.05 em).
- **Body:** DM Sans Regular (400) and Medium (500).
- **Specs / badges / labels:** JetBrains Mono Regular (400). Wide letter-spacing (0.15 to 0.25 em). Always uppercase.

Mono font is non-negotiable for technical callouts (RTU, RTS, ISO 8362-1, GMP, 503A, 503B). It is
part of the brand voice.

### Geometry

- Sharp corners only. Max `border-radius: 2px`.
- Card borders: 1 px lime at 25 to 40 percent opacity.
- Hex pattern motif (subtle, 30 to 40 percent opacity) is reserved for section dividers and dark
  backgrounds. Do not put hex pattern on every asset.

### Photography direction

- Lab and clean-room settings, vial close-ups, fill-finish machine in motion, QA in progress,
  warehouse, packaging lines.
- Dark or near-black backgrounds with **one warm light source** (think tungsten or amber rim light)
  and lime green accent glow on edges where natural.
- **No people** unless it is a gloved hand placing a vial on a tray. Hands are fine, faces are not.
- **No stock pharma clichés**: no doctors in white coats smiling, no pills scattered on a desk, no
  heart-attack-red logos.
- Composition: tight, dramatic, editorial. Think medical magazine, not B2B brochure.
- Real numbers, real specs, real packaging in frame. We do not photograph abstract metaphors.

## 5. Content Categories, Deeper

### A. Product hero (6 / month)

Tight, editorial product shots. One product in frame. Dark background. Warm rim light, lime accent
where the product catches light. Examples:

- A single 10 ml clear vial upright, stopper in foreground, aluminum seal mid-uncrimp.
- A row of amber vials in a tray, lime accent on the seal crimps.
- A 20 mm stopper sitting on brushed steel, soft shadow.
- The fill-finish machine nozzle hovering over a vial array, ISO 5 laminar flow visible as a faint haze.

Use the existing site photography (`vials-packaging-v2.webp`, `about-pharma-v3.webp`) as a
reference for lighting tone. Match it.

### B. Spec callout (4 / month)

Pure graphic / type posts. Dark forest background, lime accent border, mono-font stats centered.

Template variables:

- Headline (DM Sans Bold, white)
- Stat 1 (JetBrains Mono, lime, large)
- Stat 2 (JetBrains Mono, lime, large)
- Caption (DM Sans Regular, muted grey-green)

Examples:

- "**FILL ACCURACY** / **±0.5%** / **ISO 5 COMPATIBLE** / **40–50 VIALS / MIN**"
- "**DOCUMENTATION** / **COA · MSDS · TSE/BSE** / **STERILITY PER LOT**"
- "**SIZES** / **2 ML — 100 ML** / **CLEAR + AMBER** / **RTU + RTS**"

### C. Process / workflow (3 / month)

Step-by-step carousels showing the fill-finish journey. 3 to 5 slides per carousel. Each slide is
one frame of the process.

Suggested sequence:

1. Empty vial tray on conveyor
2. Vial under fill nozzle, mid-fill
3. Stopper being placed
4. Aluminum seal being crimped
5. Finished, capped, sealed vial in tray, with lime accent light on seal

Each slide carries a step number (mono font, top-left) and a one-line caption (DM Sans, bottom).

### D. Compliance / certification (3 / month)

Badge-led posts. Forest dark background. Lime outlined rectangles containing mono-font certification
text. Pair with a 1-line value statement in DM Sans.

Examples:

- "**FDA-REGISTERED IMPORTER OF RECORD** / Every shipment cleared at the border before it leaves our dock."
- "**ISO 8362-1** / Glass vials tested to the international standard for injectable packaging."
- "**ISO 13485** / Medical-device quality management, audited annually."

### E. Customer quote / testimonial (2 / month)

Portrait-oriented. Dark background. Large white quote text in DM Sans (semi-bold). Attribution in
Barlow Condensed (small caps, lime). Optional: very subtle, out-of-focus lab image as backdrop at 25
percent opacity.

Examples already on core-distro.com you can mine:

- Daniel Carter, Quality & Compliance Lead
- Sarah Mitchell, Director of Pharmacy Services
- Michael Anderson, Operations Manager

Always get written permission before using a real name and title. Use anonymous
"Quality Lead, 503B Pharmacy, TX" if permission is not in hand.

### F. Educational / industry insight (3 / month)

Carousels or single posts explaining a regulation, a workflow, or a comparison.

Topics:

- 503A vs 503B — what is the difference, what does each one actually buy
- USP <797> and <800> — what changed in 2023 and what compounding pharmacies must do now
- DSCSA — the serialization deadline and what it means for your supply chain
- RTU vs RTS vials — when to use which, and the cost tradeoffs
- Why aluminum seal color coding matters (the five standard colors and what they signal)

Format: 4 to 6 carousel slides. Slide 1 hooks ("DSCSA hits November 2026. Are you ready?"). Slides
2 to 5 explain. Slide 6 is CTA: "Read the full breakdown on the blog → link in bio."

### G. Behind-the-scenes (2 / month)

Stories (9:16) plus a feed post. Real warehouse, real packing line, real QA inspection. No staged
shots. Gloved hands, steel tables, inspection lamps, stack of packaged vials ready to ship.

These build trust with buyers who are tired of polished stock photos. They want to see how the
product actually leaves the dock.

### H. Campaign / seasonal / promo (2 / month)

Examples:

- Q4 FDA calendar reminder ("USP <800> review due dates")
- New product launch (when a new vial size or stopper spec lands)
- Trade-show presence ("Meet us at [event]")
- Holiday hours / shipping cutoff reminders in December
- Webinar or live Q&A announcement

## 6. Visual Do and Do Not

### Do

- Use lime as the single accent in every composition. It is a signal.
- Use mono font for every spec, stat, badge, and certification. Always uppercase.
- Pair Barlow Condensed (display) with DM Sans (body) on every text-led post.
- Match the existing site photography tone (dark, warm rim light, editorial close-ups).
- Leave negative space. We are not afraid of empty corners.
- Keep subjects centered or rule-of-thirds. Do not place text where it fights the subject.

### Do not

- Do not use red, orange, blue, or any non-brand color. The palette is forest green, lime, white, mint.
- Do not use drop shadows, glow effects, or beveled edges. Flat or near-flat only.
- Do not use rounded corners beyond 2 px radius.
- Do not photograph people, faces, or hands without gloves in clinical settings.
- Do not use stock-photo doctors, pills, or stethoscopes.
- Do not put logos of any third party on imagery without written permission.
- Do not invent specs or numbers. If a stat is not on the website or in the COA, it does not go on a post.
- **Stock imagery is now an approved initiative.** Sourced stock photography is the deliberate direction, not a compromise — see section 11. What must still never happen is a stock image presented as our own product, or an invented product rendering.
- Do not use exclamation marks in copy. The tone is confident, not excited.

## 10. The Single Most Important Thing

The buyer is a compounding pharmacy operator. They have been burned by suppliers who do not
deliver on spec, on time, or with proper documentation. Our imagery has to communicate **quiet
competence** before it communicates anything else. No hype. No flash. Lime green means we are awake
and paying attention. Forest green means we are stable. Mono font means we know the spec.

If a design makes the reader feel like they have just met a vendor who has their act together, it is
right. If it makes them feel like they have met a marketing team that is excited about itself, it is
wrong.

## 11. Stock Imagery Initiative — Unsplash

This supersedes the earlier "real photography only" line in section 6. **Stock is the plan.**

We are sourcing photography from **Unsplash** rather than running a full shoot every month. It is
faster, it is free to use under the Unsplash license, and with a strict visual filter it produces
work that matches this brand better than generic stock usually does.

**What stock covers:**

```
backgrounds and dark surfaces      lab and clean-room context
warehouse and logistics scenes     texture and material close-ups
campaign and seasonal mood         abstract editorial framing
hero and blog imagery              social post imagery across all platforms
```

**How to keep it on-brand.** Unsplash is enormous and mostly wrong for us. Every image must pass
the section 2 filter before use: dark or near-black base, one warm light source, tight editorial
composition, and palette that sits beside our greens without fighting them. **Reject anything
bright, white-background, generic-corporate, or smiling-at-camera.** A filtered miss is better than
an unfiltered hit.

**Rules that survive the change:**

```
never present a stock image as our own product, facility, or people
never composite a third-party logo onto stock imagery without written permission
never use stock to depict our specific vials, stoppers, seals or the fill-finish line
        - those are our real products and a stock lookalike is a credibility risk
        - those specific shots remain a photography shoot, when budget allows
always carry Unsplash attribution: photographer credit plus a link back, in the caption file
```

So: **stock is approved and expected for context, background, and campaign work. Our own product
photography is still ours to shoot.**

**Sourcing mechanics.** Credentials are read at use time from
`~/.config/unsplash/credentials` and are never copied into a report, a file, or a commit. Agent
guidance lives in `.agents/skills/core-distro-imagery/SKILL.md`.
