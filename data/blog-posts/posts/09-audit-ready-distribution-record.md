An audit-ready record is not simply a longer form. It is a record that helps a reviewer understand what transaction or event it concerns, which parties were involved, what evidence is available and how an exception was resolved.

The GS1 US chain-of-custody guideline describes standardized event information and exchange among supply-chain participants. HDA's public material identifies information management as part of healthcare distribution. These sources support thinking about connected records; they do not prescribe the fields, retention period or legal standard for an individual organization. ([GS1 US guideline](https://www.gs1us.org/industries-and-insights/by-industry/healthcare/standards-in-use/pharmaceutical/chain-of-custody); [HDA](https://www.hda.org/))

## Define the purpose and scope

Before designing a record, state what it is meant to explain, which product or transaction it concerns, which parties participate and what jurisdiction or internal process applies. Different records may serve different review purposes.

## Connect the record's elements

### Identity and parties

Use the product, sender, receiver and location references supported by the source system. The applicable identifiers depend on the process. Do not assume a single field set is suitable for every product or transaction.

### Movement and event evidence

Connect the order or shipment with the available event, handoff and receipt evidence. Where standardized event exchange is used, the GS1 guideline offers a reference for the conceptual chain-of-custody context. Validate the actual system and data fields with the organizations that own them.

### Exceptions and resolution

If an exception occurred, link it to the record and preserve the supporting information, responsible decision owner and recorded outcome. The example should reflect the organization's approved procedure, not a made-up universal disposition.

## Make records findable and consistent

Agree who owns record retrieval, access and updates. Use the organization's controlled systems and approved retention schedule. This article does not specify how long records must be kept; confirm retention and access requirements with your own compliance function.

## Illustrative example, not a compliance template

A fictional receipt record might connect a buyer's order reference, a shipment reference, the sender and receiving location, the information recorded at receipt and any exception routed for review. The actual field names, evidence and required retention are organization- and jurisdiction-specific. Treat this as a discussion aid only.

## Confirm the applicable requirements

This is a general record-design discussion, not legal or regulatory advice. Confirm required fields, retention, access and review expectations with your own compliance function and current primary sources before using it as a checklist.
