A useful inventory review asks more than how many units appear in a system. It asks what the pharmacy considers available for its workflow, what is already on order, what needs attention and who owns each unresolved question.

This is a process suggestion, not a claim about savings, shortage rates or a universal reorder formula. Anda's public page presents wholesale ordering and product access for pharmacy, hospital and clinic customers; Morris & Dickson describes services for independent pharmacies. Those first-party descriptions provide context for the buyer journey, not proof of any specific inventory outcome. ([Anda](https://www.andanet.com/); [Morris & Dickson independent pharmacies](https://www.morrisdickson.com/services/independent-pharmacies/))

## Review usable inventory, not just total units

Begin with the pharmacy's own records and procedures. Distinguish the stock the team treats as usable from product that is held, damaged, near expiry or otherwise unavailable under its process. The applicable treatment depends on the product and the pharmacy's approved procedures.

## A repeatable review sequence

### Reconcile on-hand and open-order information

Compare the inventory view with the pharmacy's receiving and purchasing records. List unresolved differences for follow-up rather than treating one system as automatically correct.

### Surface expiry and exception queues

Review the items your process flags for expiry, damage, hold or another exception. Record the issue and route it through the appropriate internal or supplier contact. Do not apply an invented threshold or a one-size-fits-all disposition.

### Assign an owner to each open item

For each question, note who is following up, what information is needed and when the next update is expected. A named owner is more useful than an exception that remains in an unassigned queue.

## Coordinate purchasing with the pharmacy's own needs

Use the pharmacy's records, purchasing policies and care workflow to decide what needs attention. This article does not set reorder points, safety-stock levels or purchasing recommendations. A distributor portal may help a buyer see ordering information, but confirm what the specific service actually displays and how current that information is.

## Questions for a distributor portal

- Can staff see order status and the available exception details?
- How can they contact the responsible support team?
- What information can be exported for internal review?
- Which data is current, and what needs confirmation with a representative?
