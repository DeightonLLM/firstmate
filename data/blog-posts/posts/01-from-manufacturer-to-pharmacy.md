A shipment label shows where a package is going. It does not show all the work that makes the handoff understandable to the people receiving it.

Pharmaceutical distribution connects manufacturers with pharmacies and other care settings. The work is more than moving a box: it involves orders, product and partner information, warehouse and transport activity, delivery confirmation and the follow-up when something does not match expectations. The Healthcare Distribution Alliance describes distribution as part of the broader healthcare supply chain, with roles in logistics, information management and access. McKesson's public distribution overview likewise describes service across pharmacies and other care settings. These are industry descriptions, not an audit of any particular distributor's performance. ([HDA](https://www.hda.org/); [McKesson](https://www.mckesson.com/business-solutions/pharmaceutical-medical-products-distribution/))

## The handoffs behind an order

### Order and product information

An order begins with a buyer and a supplier agreeing what is being requested and where it should go. Product identity, order details, partner identity and delivery location need to remain clear as the order moves through the parties involved. The exact systems and records vary; do not assume every distributor follows the same workflow.

### Warehouse, transport and delivery

Distribution operations may include receiving, storage, picking, packing, dispatch and delivery. Product handling needs depend on the product and the arrangements made by the parties. McKesson and Morris & Dickson describe logistics and handling capabilities in their own service materials; those statements explain their offerings and should not be generalized to every provider. ([McKesson](https://www.mckesson.com/business-solutions/pharmaceutical-medical-products-distribution/); [Morris & Dickson](https://www.morrisdickson.com/))

The receiving pharmacy also has a role: reconcile what arrived with what was expected, record relevant exceptions through its own process, and know who to contact when the shipment or records raise a question.

## Why visibility matters

A buyer needs a practical way to answer ordinary operational questions: What was ordered? What is the order's current status? Who owns the next response if information is missing or a delivery needs attention? Clear contacts and usable records make those questions easier to route. The answer is not necessarily a particular software platform; it is an agreed process that the people involved can follow.

## Questions to ask a distribution partner

- Which product categories and customer settings does the service cover?
- What handling capabilities are available for the products we buy?
- How are order or delivery exceptions communicated, and who owns follow-up?
- What records can the buyer access, and how are discrepancies resolved?
- Which service statements apply to our account and products, rather than to the provider's general marketing description?

## Draw the handoff for your own operation

A useful first step is to sketch the order-to-receipt path with your team. Mark the parties, information exchanged, custody transitions, evidence of receipt and escalation contacts. Then compare that map with the process your distribution partner actually provides.

This overview is educational, not a product-specific or regulatory checklist. Requirements and procedures depend on the product, parties and applicable jurisdiction. Confirm your own process with your quality and compliance functions.
